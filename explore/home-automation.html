Home automation

Talk to your house. Keep it in your house.

“Turn off the kitchen lights.” “Is the garage open?” “Is anything still on upstairs?” With Home Assistant and a model on your own gaming PC or Mac, your voice, your cameras and your kids' questions never leave home. It's the same relay race as Lumi: wake word, speech to text, a model, a voice.

How it works

1

Wake word

“Okay Nabu” is heard on the device itself. Nothing is sent until then.

2

Speech to text

Whisper, or Speech-to-Phrase for fixed commands.

3

Simple commands first

“Turn off the lights” is handled by Home Assistant itself, no AI needed.

4

The model for the rest

Your local model, calling Home Assistant's tools (tool calling).

5

Text to speech

Piper answers, starting about half a second in.

The best setup is a split one. Home Assistant runs on a small, always-on box (a Home Assistant Green, a Raspberry Pi 5 with an SSD, or a mini PC). The model runs on your gaming PC or Mac on the same network. If the PC is off, simple commands still work.

Under the hood. Home Assistant's Assist pipeline chains these stages, and each voice device can use its own pipeline. The Wyoming protocol connects outside speech services. The Ollama integration gives a local model “Control Home Assistant” through tool calling (local device control arrived in 2024.8); since 2026.8 a core llama.cpp integration talks to any OpenAI-compatible server (llama.cpp, vLLM). “Prefer handling commands locally” (2024.12) sends only unmatched requests to the model.

Newer pieces. Since 2025.10 a satellite can listen for two wake words, each tied to its own pipeline: one local-only, one with a model. AI Task (2025.7) lets automations ask a model for structured output, with camera snapshots attached. MCP client and server arrived in 2025.2; 2026.10 adds an “AI” settings page with a one-click MCP card for Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor (by default everything is exposed, to admins only). 2026.9 prefixed tool names (intent__HassTurnOn), so update custom prompts that name tools. The old wyoming-satellite was archived in January 2026 in favor of Linux Voice Assistant, and Piper moved to OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl.

Which models work

Below about 4 billion parameters, reliability falls off a cliff. The practical range on an 8–16 GB machine is 8–14B, or gpt-oss-20b on 16 GB.

From the Home LLM leaderboard (updated 2026-10-05): 460 deliberately hard requests and a 196-request set with fewer devices. Small samples, so differences of a few points don't mean much. Check what fits your machine.

What “88%” means at home. About one request in eight goes wrong: the wrong light, a made-up answer about a sensor. Fine for lights and music. Not fine for a lock, which is why the guardrails below matter.

Gotchas

  • Every device you expose goes into the prompt. Home Assistant suggests fewer than 25 while experimenting; 30 devices can be over a thousand tokens, read on every request.
  • Keep the model loaded. Ollama unloads a model after 5 minutes by default, so the next command waits for a reload. Set keep-alive to -1.
  • Context size. Ollama's default is 4K tokens; Home Assistant asks for 8K. More context, more memory.
  • Turn “think” off. Reasoning adds seconds and rarely helps with “turn on the porch light”.
  • Names matter more than the model. Most wrong-device mistakes come from vague names. Use areas, aliases and clear descriptions on scripts.

A weekend starter path

For a gaming PC (8 GB or more) or a Mac (16 GB or more), and a few smart lights or plugs you already have.

  1. Install Ollama on the PC or Mac and try a model: ollama run qwen3:8b (or qwen3:14b with 12 GB or more). Chat with it to feel the speed.
  2. Let your network reach it: set OLLAMA_HOST=0.0.0.0:11434 and OLLAMA_KEEP_ALIVE=-1, restart Ollama, and allow port 11434 on your home network only.
  3. Install Home Assistant on a Home Assistant Green, a Raspberry Pi 5 with an SSD, a small mini PC, or a virtual machine.
  4. Add the Ollama integration: Settings → Devices & services → Add → Ollama. Use http://<your-pc-ip>:11434, pick the model, turn on Control Home Assistant, set the context window to 8K, leave “think” off.
  5. Make it the voice assistant's brain: Settings → Voice assistants. Pick the Ollama agent and turn on Prefer handling commands locally.
  6. Expose 5–10 devices: lights, a thermostat, a speaker. Give them clear names and areas. Don't expose locks or garage doors yet.
  7. Test by typing in the Assist dialog: “turn off the kitchen lights”, “is anything on upstairs?”. Then add the Whisper and Piper add-ons and talk to it from the phone app, or a Home Assistant Voice Preview Edition speaker.
Prefer an app to commands? LM Studio can serve a model on your network too, and Home Assistant's llama.cpp integration talks to any OpenAI-compatible server.

The next level

Every room

Voice satellites

ESPHome boards or Linux Voice Assistant on a Pi Zero 2W in each room, with speech running on your GPU box. Your own wake word with microWakeWord.

Two wake words

Local-only, and smart

One wake word for fast, fixed commands with no model, another for open questions to your model.

Tools

MCP both ways

Let your house use outside tools (MCP client), or let Claude or Cursor drive your house (MCP server). Narrow what you expose.

Cameras

Frigate + a vision model

Local object detection, then a vision model writes what it saw: “A delivery driver left a package at the front door.” Frigate 0.18 added a llama.cpp provider.

Automations

AI Task

A morning briefing from your calendar, the weather and sensors; a camera snapshot turned into a sentence.

Hybrid

A frontier fallback

There's no automatic switch, but you can add a second agent or wake word that uses a cloud model for questions your local one can't handle. Only for what you're happy to send out.

Guardrails

Local keeps cameras, microphones and kids' voices at home, and works when the internet is down. It doesn't make a model careful.

1

Expose as little as possible

No locks, garage doors or alarms for the model, or only “lock” and “close”.

2

Confirm risky actions

Expose a script instead of the device, with a confirmation question, a phone notification or a PIN.

3

Never put Ollama on the internet

It has no password. Security researchers found over a thousand Ollama servers open to the world. Keep it on your network, or use a VPN like Tailscale.

4

Treat outside text as untrusted

In 2025 researchers hid instructions in a calendar invite and made an AI assistant open shutters and turn on a boiler. Calendar text, emails, device names and camera descriptions can all carry instructions. Give summary agents no control.

5

MCP: admins only, narrow, HTTPS

The MCP server hands an AI app the keys. Expose only what it needs.

6

Respect the people in the house

Use the hardware mute switch, keep cameras and the model on your network, and agree on rules for kids' rooms.
