The hardware: one box, no cloud.

The brain

NVIDIA DGX Spark

GB10 chip, 128 GB of unified memory. Runs speech-to-text, the language model and the voice, all at once.

  • Shotgun mic, aimed at the visitor, back to the crowd
  • Speakerphone as her speaker
  • USB QR scanner: it types like a keyboard
  • A big screen running Chrome in kiosk mode
Beginner

Everything you saw ran on that one computer.

Intermediate

Three model servers, each speaking the OpenAI API, on one machine.

Expert

The kiosk app is stdlib-only Python: no numpy, no evdev library, nothing to break.

Hearing: when is harder than what.

Parakeet turns speech into text very well. Knowing when a visitor has finished, in a crowd, is the hard part.

Beginner

She listens to how loud the room is, all the time. You have to be clearly louder than the room.

Intermediate

Start at 4× the room level, keep going above 2×, stop after a short silence. “Hi Lumi” also starts a visit.

Expert

Room level = 20th percentile of the last 1.5 s, re-measured continuously; a turn must peak above a fixed floor, too.

Thinking: a cheat sheet and a personality.

The system prompt (same for everyone)

Persona, event facts, every room, every session, lunch menus, the building, nearby restaurants.

The first message (just you)

Your name, the time, your next sessions, and what you talked about last time, if you said yes.

Beginner

The AI only knows what we tell it. So we tell it everything about this event.

Intermediate

She adds little tags to her answers, like [show:route:R102], that tell the screen what to show.

Expert

Identical system prompt = reusable prompt cache. Per-visitor data in the system prompt would make every visitor wait.

01

The 21-second hello

Nobody waits 21 seconds at a kiosk.

What happened

21.6 s to the first word: the model re-read thousands of tokens of event data for every visitor.

The fix

One identical prompt, warmed at startup, with cache checkpoints in the right place: ~0.5 s.

02

The bug that passed eleven reviews

Lip sync broke on every multi-sentence reply.

What happened

Queued sentences start in the future. A symmetric “sanity” clamp threw their timing away.

The fix

Asymmetric bounds (stale 5 s, ahead 60 s), and a review of the whole branch, not just each task.

03

One voice in a crowd

Quiet room: perfect. Hallway: she answered strangers.

What happened

Fixed loudness thresholds. The crowd kept starting turns.

The fix

Replayed 75 s of real crowd noise while tuning. Visitor peaks 6,000–19,000 vs. crowd ≤ 1,833. Shotgun mic, back to the crowd.

04

The computer that turned itself off

Twice. No logs. No warning.

What happened

Sustained AI load caused power spikes on older firmware. Hard power-off.

The fix

GPU clock capped at boot by a service; test load kept to one request at a time.

05

QR codes vs. screens

Badges on a screen took forever to scan.

What happened

Glare and screen refresh confuse the scanner.

The fix

Printed on matte labels, ~5 cm, highest error correction. Instant reads.

06

The silent speaker

She was talking. Nobody could hear her.

What happened

The USB speaker came up at 0% volume after every reboot.

The fix

Volume set at startup, plus a self-check that fails on a muted speaker. Rehearse on real hardware.
